Know your invader

Common cockroaches in the Phoenix Valley

Indoor and outdoor species behave differently. The right treatment depends on which one you have.

Most common

American Cockroaches

The large, reddish-brown roach often called a "sewer roach." They live in drains and utility lines and push into homes through plumbing, especially in summer heat.

Indoor

German Cockroaches

Small, tan, and fast-breeding. They live almost entirely indoors, usually in kitchens and bathrooms, and often arrive in boxes, bags, or used appliances.

Outdoor

Turkestan Cockroaches

Common across the Southwest. They nest in irrigation boxes, block walls, and mulch beds, and wander inside under doors and through weep holes.

Moisture

Oriental Cockroaches

Dark, slow-moving roaches drawn to damp, cool areas such as water meters, crawl spaces, and leaky pipes. Activity often rises after monsoon rains.

Hitchhiker

Brown-Banded Cockroaches

Small roaches that prefer warm, dry spots high up, like inside electronics, cabinets, and picture frames. They spread through the whole home rather than staying in one room.

Not sure which one?

Our Approach

Why store-bought sprays don't solve a roach problem

Sprays kill the roaches they touch, but most of the population stays hidden in places a can can't reach. Some species also learn to avoid treated areas, and the problem simply moves to another room.

We start with an inspection to find harborage areas, entry points, and the moisture or food sources keeping them around. Then we combine targeted baits, crack-and-crevice treatments, and exterior protection to reduce the population and keep new roaches out.

Targeted gel baits: placed where roaches feed and shelter

Crack-and-crevice treatment: reaches wall voids, cabinets, and appliance gaps

Exterior perimeter protection: stops outdoor roaches before they get inside

Prevention guidance: simple fixes for drains, moisture, and entry points

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Everything Phoenix Valley homeowners ask about cockroach control.

Q Why am I seeing cockroaches if my house is clean?
Cleanliness helps, but it isn't the whole story. Cockroaches need water and shelter as much as food, and in the Phoenix Valley they often come up through drains or in from irrigated landscaping. A clean home can still have leaky pipes, open weep holes, or gaps under doors that let them in.
Q Are cockroaches harmful to my family?
Cockroaches can carry bacteria on their bodies and contaminate food and surfaces as they move. Their droppings and shed skins are also a known trigger for allergies and asthma, especially in children. That's why we treat infestations promptly and thoroughly.
Q How long does it take to get rid of cockroaches?
Most homes see a noticeable drop in activity within the first one to two weeks. Indoor species like German cockroaches can take longer because they breed quickly, so follow-up visits may be needed. We'll set expectations based on the species and the size of the infestation.
Q Are your cockroach treatments safe for kids and pets?
Yes. We use targeted baits and crack-and-crevice applications that place product where roaches live, not on open surfaces. We'll walk you through any simple precautions before and after treatment.
Q What can I do to prevent cockroaches from coming back?
Fix leaks, keep drains covered or flushed, store food in sealed containers, and avoid leaving pet food out overnight. Sealing gaps around pipes, doors, and weep holes makes a big difference too. We'll point out the specific risk areas on your property during the visit.
